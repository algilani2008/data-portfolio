# Findings (computed by run_analysis.py, latest common year 2024)

- **Low-carbon leader:** United Arab Emirates produced 31.7% of its electricity from low-carbon sources in 2024 (nuclear 22.9% + solar 8.6%). The other five GCC countries were all at or below 4.9%, versus 40.9% for the world.
- **Saudi Arabia:** oil's share of electricity fell from 52.8% (2015) to 34.5% (2024); low-carbon share rose from 0.01% to 2.16%. Generation grew 5.1% a year since 2000 (139 -> 455 TWh).
- **Growth mostly met by fossil fuels:** of the extra electricity produced across the GCC between 2015 and 2024 (219 TWh), 33% came from low-carbon sources. By country: Saudi Arabia 9%, United Arab Emirates 112%, Qatar 14%, Kuwait 8%, Oman 15%, Bahrain 1%. A value above 100% means low-carbon output grew by more than total demand, so fossil-fuel generation actually fell.
- **Per person:** every GCC country generated more electricity per person than the world average (3,790 kWh): from 9,297 kWh (Oman) to 23,615 kWh (Bahrain), i.e. 2.5x to 6.2x.
- **Carbon intensity:** grams of CO2-equivalent per kWh ranged from 468 (United Arab Emirates) to 902 (Bahrain), versus 471 for the world.
